I knew I was an artist by the time I was three years old. When I was a small toddler I was falling asleep in watercolor paints and experimenting with shapes and messes and crafting and getting my hands on any type of supplies my young hands could manage. Something about the act of creating art, of making 'portals' and making people feel some type of way about whatever I had put on the page / canvas was not only enthralling for me, but it was a shared spiritual experience.

The older I grew, the more I realized that the creation of art was not this matrices’ first plan for me, or any artist’s true path, despite it being their actual purpose; their raison d'être for being here. We learn as creators that the ultimate illusion seeks to siphon our talents; feed us with lies so that we grease its machinery with our bodies, our blood, our sweat and tears. It happened to our parents, and probably their parents, and theirs before that. It’s a tragic cycle that affects us all, however art, and all types of it, is a way for us to transmute the pain and evil that embeds itself within us as we weather such a lifelong storm.

Masters of their craft, creators, artists, and everyone who falls under such an umbrella term are vulnerable to the siphoning of their gifts, their talents, and their focus. Their blood is often used as a ritualistic sacrifice to wet the gears of this awful beast; an unholy, all-devouring parasitic disease that has infected our precious planet and the mentality of our species. Through discrimination, prejudice, unforgivable abuses and transgressions against the most vulnerable and the most spiritually attuned of us, the ones who have abandoned the path in favor of alleged true power have ultimately, in my opinion, made great multidimensional sins against the human race as well as the planet, perhaps even the universe.

But balance can be found in the smallest crags. Small fissures can be made to let the light in. In the darkness of the caves, we find utmost silence to care for our minds and bodies temporarily, wherein we can nurture the wounds of our souls. Even in the pain of isolation sometimes we can pick up the pieces; or leave them behind all together and build something anew. So to speak, gardening is brutally hard work, especially in this sunlight that is becoming more intense every day.

However, planting a garden has always been hard work. Our ancestors didn’t innately know how to plant their seeds of good fortune. They turned inward. They saw God and the Goddess and the void within themselves and faced them as well as the shadow that dwells there; again and again, evolution would take place. Survival was not just a means of moving forward. It was moving backward, to the present, forward, and back again. Even scientists are learning that time and even space is indeed not as linear as we once thought.

And, how does all of this factor into our identities as artists? Art brings about the portals to other lives, realities, destinies, modalities of thought, etc. We are literally, and figuratively, masters of manifesting and spiritual architects. Our visions move mountains and refill the seas. Before a scientist made it possible, an artist dreamed about it and passed it along to them. It is our inspiration and muse that will terra form the earth back to the health it so desperately needs. Perhaps our people as well.

My entire life I did not know my worth as an artist, and I find this a common thread and story among people who create for a living and for enjoyment. They know not their talent, the depth of their skills as storytellers and record keepers. Their lore is exponentially more than mere exposition. It is the culmination of their multidimensional experiences in this bridge-world, along with molecular memory of ancestral ritual practices from times long forgotten.
